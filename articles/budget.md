A budget is not about cutting out everything you enjoy. It is a plan for where your money goes, so that you decide, instead of finding out at the end of the month. Here is a simple approach that works for most households.

## Step 1: Track one month

Before you set any limits, find out where your money is actually going. Go through the last 30 days of bank and card statements and sort every transaction into a few groups: housing, utilities, food, transport, debt payments, and everything else. Most people find at least one surprise.

## Step 2: List your fixed bills

Write down the bills that are roughly the same every month: rent or mortgage, insurance, phone, internet, car payment, minimum debt payments. These come first. Add up the total and compare it with your take-home pay.

## Step 3: Pick a simple framework

Two popular approaches:

- **50/30/20.** Roughly 50% of take-home pay goes to needs, 30% to wants and 20% to savings and extra debt payments. It is a guide, not a rule. If rent alone takes half your income, adjust the numbers.
- **Zero-based budgeting.** Every dollar gets a job before the month starts, until income minus planned spending equals zero. It takes more effort but gives you the most control.

## Step 4: Plan for the irregular stuff

Car registration, holidays, school supplies and annual subscriptions break budgets because they do not arrive every month. Add them up for the year, divide by 12, and set that amount aside each month.

## Keep it going

- **Check in weekly.** Ten minutes on a Sunday is enough to see if you are on track.
- **Use the tool you will actually use.** A spreadsheet, an app or a notebook all work.
- **Adjust without guilt.** Your first budget will be wrong in places. Change the numbers and keep going.

## When the numbers do not add up

If your essential bills are more than your income, a budget alone will not fix it. That is the time to look at assistance programs, call your creditors about hardship options, or speak to a nonprofit credit counselor.
