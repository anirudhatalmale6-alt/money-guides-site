When the weather turns cold, heating bills can climb faster than paychecks. If you are already behind, or worried you soon will be, the **Low Income Home Energy Assistance Program (LIHEAP)** is one of the first places to look.

## What LIHEAP is

LIHEAP is a federally funded program that is run locally by each state, many tribes and the U.S. territories. The money comes from Washington, but your state decides the details: who qualifies, how much help is available and when applications open.

Depending on where you live, LIHEAP can help with:

- **Regular heating or cooling bills**, usually paid straight to your utility company
- **Energy crises**, such as a shut-off notice or running out of heating oil or propane
- **Repairs** to an unsafe or broken furnace, and in some states minor weatherization work

## Who qualifies

Each state sets its own income limit, within federal rules. Many states use a figure around 150% of the federal poverty guidelines, and some go higher. Households that already receive SNAP, SSI or TANF are treated as eligible automatically in some states.

You usually do not need to be behind on your bill to apply. Priority often goes to households with older adults, people with disabilities and young children.

## How to apply

1. **Find your local office.** Applications are handled by a state agency or a local community action agency. The federal Administration for Children and Families lists a contact for every state.
2. **Gather your documents.** Expect to show proof of income for everyone in the household, a recent energy bill, ID and your address.
3. **Apply early.** LIHEAP money is limited and is handed out until it runs out. In many states the heating season window opens in the fall.

## If you already have a shut-off notice

Tell the agency straight away. Crisis assistance is often processed faster than regular help. Call your utility company too: many states have winter rules that limit disconnections during cold weather, and most utilities offer payment plans if you ask.

## The bottom line

LIHEAP will not cover every bill, but it can take real pressure off during the most expensive months of the year. If money is tight, it is worth applying even if you are not sure you qualify. The worst outcome is a "no", and many people who assume they earn too much are wrong.
