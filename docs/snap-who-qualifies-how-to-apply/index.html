SNAP Food Benefits: Who Qualifies and How to Apply

By Pocketwise Staff · Oct 28, 2026 · 2 min read

The Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP), still widely known as food stamps, helps low-income households buy groceries. Benefits are loaded onto an EBT card each month and can be used at most supermarkets, many convenience stores and a growing number of farmers' markets.

The income tests

SNAP is run by each state under federal rules, so details vary. In general, a household has to pass two income tests:

  • Gross income (before deductions) at or below 130% of the federal poverty line
  • Net income (after allowed deductions) at or below 100% of the poverty line

Households with someone aged 60 or over, or someone with a disability, usually only need to meet the net income test. Many states use broader rules that let more working families qualify, so do not rule yourself out on the numbers alone.

Deductions that count

Net income is what is left after deductions, and they can make a big difference. Depending on your situation, these may include:

  • A standard deduction every household gets
  • 20% of earned income
  • Child care costs needed for work or school
  • Child support you pay
  • High housing and utility costs
  • Medical costs over a set amount for older or disabled members

Work rules

Some adults have to meet work requirements to keep receiving SNAP, such as working or volunteering a set number of hours a week. Who is covered by these rules, and the exemptions, changed recently and can differ by state, so check with your state agency about your situation.

How to apply

  1. Apply with your state agency, online, by phone, by mail or in person. You can file with just your name, address and signature to lock in your application date, then send the rest.
  2. Complete an interview, usually by phone.
  3. Provide proof of identity, income, rent and utilities.

States generally decide within 30 days. Households with very little income or money in the bank may qualify for expedited benefits within about 7 days.

What SNAP covers

You can buy most foods for the household, plus seeds and plants that grow food. You cannot buy hot prepared foods (with limited exceptions), alcohol, tobacco, vitamins, or non-food items like cleaning supplies and toiletries.

If your application is denied, you have the right to ask for a fair hearing. The denial letter explains how.
