Paying Down Credit Card Debt: Five Options Worth Knowing

By The Wise Dollar Staff · Oct 18, 2026 · 2 min read

Credit card interest rates are high, and a balance that only gets the minimum payment can take years to clear. There is no single right answer, but these are the main options to consider.

1. The avalanche method

List your cards by interest rate. Pay the minimum on all of them, then put every extra dollar toward the card with the highest rate. When it is paid off, move to the next one. This saves the most money in interest.

2. The snowball method

List your cards by balance instead. Put extra money toward the smallest balance first. You pay a little more interest overall, but clearing a card quickly gives you a sense of progress, which helps many people stick with it.

3. Ask your card issuer for help

Many people never try this. Call the number on the back of the card and ask:

  • Whether you can get a lower interest rate
  • Whether they have a hardship program if you have lost income or had a medical emergency

Hardship programs can temporarily lower your rate or payment. Ask how it affects your account and credit report before agreeing.

4. Nonprofit credit counseling

Nonprofit credit counseling agencies can review your budget for free or a low fee. They may suggest a debt management plan, where you make one monthly payment to the agency and it pays your creditors, often at reduced interest rates. Look for agencies that are accredited and nonprofit, and be wary of anyone pushing you into a plan before reviewing your situation.

5. Balance transfers and consolidation loans

A balance transfer card with a 0% introductory rate, or a personal loan at a lower rate than your cards, can cut interest costs. Watch for transfer fees, and what the rate becomes when the introductory period ends. These only help if you stop adding new debt to the old cards.

A word on debt settlement

Some companies offer to negotiate your debts down for less than you owe. Settlement can work for some people, but it usually damages your credit, forgiven debt may count as taxable income, and fees can be high. For debt relief services sold by phone, it is illegal for the company to charge fees before it has actually settled or changed at least one of your debts. Be very cautious of any company asking for money up front.

Whatever route you choose, the most important step is the first one: knowing exactly what you owe, to whom, and at what rate.
