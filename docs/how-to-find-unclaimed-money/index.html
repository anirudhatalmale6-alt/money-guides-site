There May Be Money Waiting in Your Name. Here Is How to Check for Free

By The Wise Dollar Staff · Oct 26, 2026 · 2 min read

Every year, money gets separated from its owners. A final paycheck that was never cashed. A utility deposit from an old apartment. A bank account opened years ago and forgotten. When a business cannot reach the owner, it eventually has to hand the money over to the state, which holds it until someone claims it.

What counts as unclaimed property

Common examples include:

  • Dormant checking and savings accounts
  • Uncashed paychecks, refund checks and rebates
  • Utility and rental deposits
  • Insurance payouts and life insurance benefits
  • Stock dividends and the contents of safe deposit boxes

How to search

Each state runs its own official unclaimed property program, and searching is free.

  1. Search your state's official website. Search for your state's name plus "unclaimed property" and make sure the address ends in .gov or is linked from the state treasurer's or comptroller's site.
  2. Try MissingMoney.com. It is endorsed by the National Association of Unclaimed Property Administrators and lets you search many states at once.
  3. Search every state you have lived or worked in, not just where you live now.
  4. Use every version of your name, including maiden names, nicknames and common misspellings.

For old U.S. savings bonds, the Treasury runs a separate free search tool called Treasury Hunt on TreasuryDirect.

Claiming money from a relative

If a parent or other relative has died, their unclaimed property may be passed to the heirs. Expect to provide a death certificate and documents showing you are entitled to the estate, such as a will or letters of administration.

Watch out for fees and scams

Claiming your own money from the state is free. Be careful with:

  • "Finders" who offer to recover your money in exchange for a percentage. It is legal in many states, but it is rarely worth it when you can file yourself.
  • Calls, texts or emails saying you have unclaimed money and asking for a fee or your bank details first. State programs do not charge to release your money.

Make it a habit

New property is reported to states every year, so a search that comes up empty today may not stay empty. Checking once a year takes a few minutes and costs nothing.
